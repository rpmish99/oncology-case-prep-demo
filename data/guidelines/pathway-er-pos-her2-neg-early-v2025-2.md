SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT. Written for a demonstration. It is a simplified, illustrative pathway and is not clinical guidance.

# Scope
Adults with stage I to II, ER-positive, HER2-negative invasive breast cancer after breast surgery.

# 1. Confirm biomarkers
1.1 Confirm ER, PR and HER2 status from the final pathology report. If another note disagrees with the pathology report, the pathology report governs, and the discrepancy is flagged for correction.
1.2 HER2 immunohistochemistry 0 or 1+ is negative. Score 2+ requires in situ hybridization.

# 2. Genomic assay (node-negative disease)
2.1 Order a 21-gene recurrence score assay for pT1b to pT2, pN0, ER-positive, HER2-negative disease.
2.2 Postmenopausal patients (or age over 50) with recurrence score 0 to 25: endocrine therapy alone. Chemotherapy is not routinely recommended.
2.3 Recurrence score 26 or higher: discuss chemotherapy followed by endocrine therapy.
2.4 Premenopausal patients age 50 or younger with recurrence score 16 to 25: discuss the added value of chemotherapy or ovarian suppression.

# 3. Endocrine therapy
3.1 Postmenopausal: an aromatase inhibitor for 5 years is the preferred starting option. Consider extended therapy after review at year 5.
3.2 Tamoxifen is an alternative when an aromatase inhibitor is not tolerated. Tamoxifen carries an increased risk of venous thromboembolism. A history of deep vein thrombosis or pulmonary embolism is a relative contraindication and requires specialist review.
3.3 Before an aromatase inhibitor: review bone density. Obtain a DEXA scan if the last one is more than 24 months old.

# 4. Bone health
4.1 Aromatase inhibitors reduce bone density. For lumbar or hip T-score of -2.0 to -2.5 (osteopenia) with an aromatase inhibitor, ensure calcium and vitamin D and repeat DEXA every 1 to 2 years.
4.2 For T-score of -2.5 or lower, or high fracture risk, consider an antiresorptive agent (bisphosphonate or denosumab).

# 5. Radiation
5.1 After lumpectomy, whole-breast radiation is recommended. Omission may be considered in selected patients age 70 or older with small, low-risk, ER-positive tumors.

# 6. Genetic counseling
6.1 Offer germline genetic counseling when any of the following applies: diagnosis at age 50 or younger; triple-negative disease; a first-degree relative with breast cancer at age 50 or younger; male breast cancer; ovarian cancer in the family. Otherwise, use shared decision-making.
